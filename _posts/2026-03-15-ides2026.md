# The Ides of March and Spring Recess
## 14 – 15 March 2026

Pi Day and The Ides of March have once again arrived, and a plethora of reasons make these two days hold especial significance for me. Both belong to the group of lesser-observed holidays that generally do not entail festivities or institutional closures. Yet it is the quiet appreciation of what days like these represent that can provide a peculiar source of bliss for their observer. This is especially true for dates of historic significance like March 15th, which marks the death of Gaius Julius Caesar in 44 B.C. From reminding friends and family of what occurred in the Theatre of Pompey over 2069 years ago to duly appreciating the versatility of pi and its conceptually simple derivation the day before, perhaps I appreciate these holidays for all that they leave one to do out of the ordinary; that is, to simply reflect upon what they mean.

The Ides of March further coincide with what I consider to safely be the end of winter. The grass has become remarkably green since the snow has melted this year, and a relatively warm temperature well above 0º C is reached nearly every day. This weblog post serves to commemorate March 15th for these reasons. Yet, this year, the Ides also marks an especially eventful weekend at the head of my first spring recess in college.

I reached the Middlesex Fells reservation in 39 minutes by bike yesterday and was able to enjoy the serene trails in the early afternoon without the fear of sunset. Two realizations came upon me on the trail that I will record here. I had resolved to carry a paper map to every hiking location that I am not completely familiar with on my last visit to Middlesex Fells at the same trailhead. Forgetting to do so yesterday has crystallized this notion as principle. Besides the freedom from reliance on a cell phone to display a map, there is a joy in navigating—and perhaps marking up—a physical representation of the land that is being traversed.

What I realized some time afterwards may be thanks to an online source that I recently found. It addresses a phenomenon that I have noticed on many of my return trips from the Beaver Brook North Reservation—a view of what I now know is the [Boston Basin]( https://www.bostonbasinhills.org/). While there was no striking view of the city below on my Middlesex Fells route yesterday, it was if I could sense more clearly that I was ascending out of the basin in a manner similar to that described on the website. The increasingly alpine-like terrain that is characteristic of Middlesex Fells made this effect more pronounced.

I have archived this source along with numerous other hyperlinks and files of interest on a new page of this website. With spring recess spanning the entirety of next week (which I would have preferred to enjoy at the end of the month), one of my plans involves reading some of the resources I listed in more detail. While the academic papers and the excerpts of French literature that I have read for coursework have been thoroughly enjoyable, I have neglected reading for productive leisure. The wholesome bliss of reading great works has just come into memory, however, reminding me of a practice which would be beneficial to maintain beyond my reading for this coming week. God has bestowed yet another Ides of March upon me that, I pray, I will have been able to enjoy in its entirety by tomorrow morning—and unlike the man who ended the largest republic of the ancient world.
