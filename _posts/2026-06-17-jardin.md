# Crossing the Threshold of Adjustment in the Jardin de Reuilly - Paul Pernin
## 14 – 16 June 2026

Two weeks have now passed since I departed for Paris. Between boarding the first aeroplane flight to Detroit airport and this warm evening storeys above the streets of the 13th arrondissement, I have observed and experienced so much that deserves careful reflection. The purpose of this piece is not to recount my voyage and my long, tiring fortnight past, however. It would be infeasible to do so even if the time permitted. I am instead writing most immediately after a conspicuous and seemingly instantaneous return to a normal state of being that struck me this past Friday. Recording this revelation may prove useful for my future reference as well as for those who find themselves thrown in the depths of disquietude when navigating a similar unfamiliarity. Yet this peace of mind also provides a focal point though which the first two weeks of my sojourn in Paris may be provided with the record that they deserve. If time permits this evening, I intend to refine divers observations that are worthy of being recorded here.

My reader may have been struck by the fact that I am not writing this weblog entry in French, as I initially resolved to restrain myself to do in order to ensure “complete immersion.” Among the innumerable lessons learned over this long stretch of fourteen days, I have found the need for periodic breaks from the language that I am immersed in. This most directly concerns *thinking* in French instead of my mother tongue. For, after long enough, a certain fatigue seems to render this thinking into no thinking at all. The depth and clarity of thought achieved in English does not seem accessible. A form of a “break” that is provided by returning to English may be just as necessary to fruitful learning as reserving a break is in other endeavours. This is precisely why English weblog entries will continue; my energy and *time* may instead be focused on the reflection that this past fortnight demands. The resulting clarity of thought may yet benefit my study of French in ways unforeseen.

These resolutions have only come into being after my realisation in the Jardin de Reuilly - Paul Pernin on Friday evening. Manifold factors have likely contributed to my consternations that began shortly after arriving in Paris. The aeroplane journey was even worse than I had anticipated. The forgotten pain of sitting in an airplane seat precluded any quality sleep, the change of pressure combined with sinusitis caused frightful nosebleeds, and my resulting physical state necessitated the search for medical care in Paris. I continue to find the influence that an illness can have on all aspects of life remarkable. Yet one of the most consequential causes of unease—one that I have only identified this weekend—has been the mindset with which I entered the capital.

This mindset may most broadly be described as the expectation of being able to settle into the routine workload and rhythm of the university semester. Although the two courses that I am taking with the study abroad programme can be fatiguing, the workload beyond class time has been lighter than expected. Yet the time and energy required to navigate new environments in the midst of a city of this size, in the midst of the work week, has fuelled my disquietudes. This phenomenon likely peaked after a class visit to the Louvre this past Wednesday morning. The time-consuming ordeal of exiting the museum and *commuting* to French tutoring not only seemed to be burning valuable time to do work (regardless of how relatively light of a course workload I had for that day), but navigating the unfamiliar expanses of the Louvre grounds and the surrounding Parisian streets must been particularly taxing on the mind. I have written before on the bane of the commute and the loathsome amount of lost time accumulated by a long commute. Indeed, travelling within the perpetual *affluence* on public transit near the centre of the capital may be the apotheosis of the stressful commute. These disquietudes were all situated in the context of my coursework, however. My sojourn is not a vacation, and the underlying stress of each day seemed to threaten my purpose for being here—to progress towards French fluency as rapidly and effectively as possible. 

This is all to premise what became clear to me in Jardin de Reuilly - Paul Pernin on Friday afternoon. This would be an unusual excursion for a working day during the normal semester. Yet I realised before departing to the park that my days in Paris cannot be treated as those of the fall or spring term. Beyond the gruelling process of familiarisation with the city, however, lies the fact that there is simply not enough work to treat my days here as such.

It first came upon me after reciting phonetic practices in the park that the consternations I had regarding my coursework were not warranted. In other words, I must have perceived both the workload and the stakes of the two classes I am taking as greater than they are. From this moment, I internalised that I will nevertheless continue to learn as much French as possible. I not only realised that my efforts to learn through and beyond the courses were working. But the almost passive manner in which one acquires the language through natural exposure may actually make enough studying outside of focused class time counterproductive.

The observations that I have made thus far may sound superficial or excessively personal to concern my reader. Yet for one in a state of uncertainty and apprehension for almost two weeks, the return to an ease of mind and a joy in learning renders this careful account worth the time and the effort. If nothing else, this piece may serve as a marker of this turning point in my study abroad experience and of my gratitude unto God.

###

I had the ability to complete a long-awaited familiarisation with the heart of this city yesterday afternoon. A visit to *la Crypte Archéologique de la Cité* to view Roman ruins of Lutèce and artifacts of the Parisii, followed by [this walking tour](https://parisjetaime.com/article/au-coeur-des-iles-parisiennes-a702), finally allowed me to orient myself in a certain manner. The unfathomable urban immensity surrounding me for the past two weeks could henceforth be grounded in the small islet from which it all began.

This piece may disproportionately cover personal reflections, leaving many of the more general observations I intend to share for later writing. Indeed, there remains much to reflect upon from this foray into life in Paris. Developing weblog entries that do justice to these observations may prove wholesome throughout the next month. To be able to approach this coming week with such joy and excitement is remarkable given my state of mind only two days before now. One of the points that I understood in the sermon delivered this morning was that God’s means of grace are almost never immediately conveyed.

## 16 – 17 June 2026

A brief addendum to this piece following the beginning of this week is necessary. In spite of the onset of another illness, the general ease of being that I am now in continues to be remarkable. It is now clearer to me than ever that it may have simply required a fortnight to adjust to a life and study routine. While this observation appears relatively simple, I have only truly arrived to it after several days in a normal state of mind. The fatigue that I experienced at the end of each day throughout the past two weeks, even after having recovered from an illness, has made another phenomenon clear. One must never underestimate the ability of underlying stress throughout the day to cause fatigue. I leave my reader to refer to [this resource from the University of Michigan]( https://global.umich.edu/resilient-travel-home/) that is intended to build resilient travelling skills. I can finally add that, after surmounting the despair of travel, I am beginning to perceive the bliss of living and learning the French language in the capital.
