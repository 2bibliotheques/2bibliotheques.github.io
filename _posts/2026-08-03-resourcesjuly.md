# Resources and Reflections from July: Language Learning and Personal Documentation

## 1 – 3 August 2026

It is often easy to read extremely good advice without internalising its significance or its meaning. I have found this to be especially noticeable when reading advice that I could second to others only after having learned from experience how true it may be. The lesson that one learns overcoming challenges are often learned the deepest, and these lessons are almost always most appreciated following great travail. This holds especially true in endeavours where efficiency is often a function of idiosyncratic needs, preferences, and learning styles. Acquiring a new language is a prime example. Yet after continuing to study French following one-and-one-half months of complete language immersion in Paris, I have found certain documented advice to be compelling enough to share here. It is hoped that those who are attempting to master another language may find something valuable from this entry that does not require studying abroad to deeply understand and appreciate.

In the first part of this entry, I will share two sets of language learning advice—one from an online resource that I have recently found and one that I have produced whilst learning French in Paris. Although both resources are in French, the advice is so valuable and so applicable to those learning other languages that running each through a translator will prove well worth one’s time. The cognitive boons and the wholesome joy of acquiring another tongue are beyond the scope of this piece. Yet it is hoped that my readers who are not studying a foreign language will find that many of the pedagogical or practical points contained in the two resources apply to almost any life endeavour. If not, the pragmatism of personal documentation that I will put forth in the second part of this entry applies to practically any inhabitant of the modern world.

## Two French Resources for Learning Any Language

With that said, the remarkable degree of comfort and *naturalness* in engaging with the French language that I have noted in the previous entry has continued despite being broken from immersion for several weeks. My studies of a few unknown or shallowly understood grammatical topics seem to be much clearer, oftentimes reinforced by my daily consumption of French video content that follows in the evening. As my tutor for the studying abroad program noted during one of our sessions, learning French is not like studying mathematics. Only now do I fully understand how that is so. This is why, to my reader who may be studying a foreign tongue on his own, I implore—continue. In those moments when one’s language studies seem inefficient or like a waste of time, there is an assurance that I can now attest to. Previously studied grammatical minutiae of a language will resurface remarkably easily when one is further along the learning curve, and it may then be mastered thanks to this previous exposure. 

Complete immersion in a language must be the most effective and profound way to develop language studies into the pursuit of mastery. Although one-and-one-half months was enough in my case to cause the paradigm shift that every learner may eventually experience, even a few days of complete immersion exponentiates one’s progress in a language when the immersion is embraced. I advise my reader to consider the importance of this immersion along with the value of every preceding hour of studying that enables one to take complete advantage of his time abroad. Of course, I am well aware that not every one of my readers will have the opportunity to experience complete foreign langue immersion in the near future.

These readers may find the following resource especially useful. I can most immediately attest to the usefulness of this resource to those who possess a similar level of French fluency to me and who have recently had an experience of complete immersion. But following this advice when studying in an anglophone country could save hours of time for other learners. An optimal language studying routine is highly individualised, and along with the plethora of other lived experiences in learning French that I could share here, it would be better if my reader referred to a five-day language “course” that comes in the form of video lessons and short exercises.

## On Personal Documentation

It is hoped that one may appreciate the unparalleled value of writing as a form of developing and fixing one’s reflections through reading this weblog. Indeed, my writings thus far have fulfilled and even exceeded the roles ascribed to them in the [introduction to this web site](https://2bibliotheques.github.io/2026/02/22/introduction.html). Writing in this manner is enjoyable and wholesome for the mind, and it produces materials that are ever more invaluable as time passes. Taking the initiative to produce careful personal documentation in other, perhaps more mundane forms will similarly accrue in value. Through sharing several examples of my own personal documentation that I now appreciate greatly, I will clarify the sort of records that I am referring to. These examples also show how the tedium and seeming unproductivity when first establishing some of these records will be overshadowed as they become a practical and valuable asset almost immediately afterwards.

One of the most tedious of my personal documents to establish is now one of the easiest to maintain. In anticipation of accumulating a vast collection of books and physical media over my lifetime, I developed a catalogue of my “personal library” when the time this endeavour would require presented itself over last winter recess. Every book, DVD, CD, and Blu-Ray disc that I own was recorded in a Microsoft Excel sheet over the course of several days. This record includes a simple catalogue numbering system from which one may determine the format of every material. The ability to determine the exact edition of a piece of media that I own along with all pertinent information relating to the object is now a matter of executing a CTRL+F command. Here is the time-saving elegance of the computer at its finest, only inferior to its paper counterpart by the need to keep and maintain backup copies. Even then, as I have learned when digitising other records, the Excel table may always be printed. A template for this catalogue is provided in a .XLSX file hyperlinked below. As a means to reinforce vocabulary, note that my catalogue has been produced in the French language.

[Le modèle du catalogue de la bibliothèque personnelle de Adam Hamilton (fichier .XLSX)]( https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1wOxYhvrsAJKrpMSv2HTzpInvCtnk84Zn/edit?usp=drive_link&ouid=114436756857963989891&rtpof=true&sd=true)

In the spirit of scientific observation, I recorded the time taken to travel to various locations in Cambridge from my dormitory throughout last semester. A well-documented median figure for walking, bike, and train travel times proves useful in facing the reality that is the commute. (Although, the duration of most weekday trips with which I would be concerned in Cambridge pales in comparison to the time one loses in extended automobile commutes. This is a topic for another piece.) With only a stopwatch and this excel sheet, one may accumulate a record of the travel times, travel dates, and other circumstances (such as bike unlocking times) of his voyages for later, irrefutable citation. A hyperlink to a simple Microsoft Excel template adapted directly from my record is provided below.

[Travel Times Template (.XLSX)]( https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=1madNti9MN8gb7mKPnMmBVbY4jzubhi1_)

Another watch of mine has made it possible to accumulate a much more information-dense record. Weekly syncing of my Garmin Forerunner 205—a running watch from 2009 that I received like new as a Christmas gift from my grandparents in 2024—has built a long log of running routes on my Garmin Connect online account. I spent an hour this week perusing and organising this list. I cleared my watch memory on the same day, keeping entries from only the past month out of those dating back to December of 2024. The memory was not cleared before I exported all of these runs as a .CSV file in order to have a backup should the Garmin Connect website become inaccessible. It is continually astounding to view information ranging from my pace on portions of the exact routes I ran to the weather conditions during the entire run. Routes from my recent sojourn in Paris and those from my holiday in the United Kingdom with my sister and brother-in-law remain there. Although I have no template to share, I can advise one who has a similar running watch to make a similar backup.

Finally, and most importantly for the exigence of this piece, I must stress the value of recording all of one’s online accounts after having recently done so myself. This is both the most valuable and potentially the most tedious personal record that I will share here. If my reader takes no action in response to this piece up to this point, let him take heed to this advice to create a similar account list. A record of online services with which one registered an account for, along with the associated emails and passwords, is an immediately useful tool for accessing each account as well as an invaluable asset in the modern data privacy landscape. The act of reviewing each of one’s online accounts is in itself a practice of personal data awareness. It provides a systematic means to delete the accounts that one no longer needs or to update the information on existing ones. The established list embodies a sort of personal information footprint that could be useful in ways yet unforeseen.

As much as one may dismiss cataloguing every online account of his as infeasible or too time-consuming, the list does not need to be created in one sitting. So often are endeavours with imposing final products a matter of incremental development over time. Further still, there are likely one or two online accounts that I have missed in my own record. But once the list is built, it need only be maintained.

My own list is organised across two Microsoft Excel sheets in one workbook. One sheet contains a list of “lower level” accounts that may be accessed with passwords that I have memorised. These passwords are simply represented by shorthand notes. The other sheet contains essential information for “higher level” accounts with complex passwords that are indexed on paper. I leave my reader with one final template below, containing with an example entry for both sheets.

## Updates to the Resources Page on This Site

Since the inception of this web site, many hyperlinks have been added to the “Resources” page that are highly useful or informative. Perhaps this page serves as yet another type of documentation that only increases in depth over time; it reflects my various web browser bookmark folders while providing more context through short descriptions of select resources. Along with all of the resources that I have shared in this entry, I have added a short documentary from the French channel ARTE that raises several extraordinary points regarding the proliferation of generative artificial intelligence in recent years. It is yet another resource that is worth my reader’s time to consult using subtitles. One of the most striking points—one that has surfaced in other French sources regarding AI that I have consulted—addresses the incredibly narrow vision of artificial intelligence in the United States that is directly linked to the consumption of computing power and the resulting rise of new data centres.

Companies such as OpenAI or Meta are vying for consumers to use their omnipotent chatbot model that consume gigawatts of energy. The author and philosopher who was interviewed in the video propounds that AI has immense potential through specialised models that are significantly computationally lighter. “Artificial intelligence” is itself a term that anthropomorphises and depoliticises the technology. A hyperlink to this video may be found [here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ASciuwRveAg).
