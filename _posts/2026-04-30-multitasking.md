# Canaday Hall, April, and the Folly of Multitasking
## 26-30 April 2026

### 26-27 April 
It has been nearly one month since my last weblog post went online. Even before committing that change to the GitHub repository, however, I had realised something on the night of “Housing Day” at Harvard College that demanded further meditation through writing. It was to my immense pleasure in the morning that I learned that I would be a resident of the Currier House student dormitory complex—as I prefer to describe it—for the next three academic years.

To have guaranteed heated room and board regardless of what dormitory I received endowed a certain light-heartedness to my preference for one in the Radcliffe Quadrangle. A measure of quietness and tranquillity being some distance from Harvard Square and Central Cambridge will nevertheless be something that I pray to enjoy. My concerns about the time that I will lose commuting between the Radcliffe Quadrangle and Harvard Yard have been ameliorated throughout this past month by repeated measurements; the entire process of readying my bicycle and travelling between Canaday Hall and Currier House via bicycle lanes and sidewalks takes no more than 10 minutes.

A dormitory open house event for rising sophomores occurred on the same night, and that is where a certain disquietude accompanying a separation from the ease of mind found at home came upon me. I then realised that I was longing to return to my hallway single room in Canaday. The thought that home could be found in what I have realised is one of the busiest and loudest parts of the Harvard campus remains peculiar to me. It also attests to the wonders worked by time—and perhaps to the efficacy of my light and sound blocking measures for quiet sleep. This episode nevertheless brought forth my appreciation for Canaday Hall and a satisfaction in being beyond content with the shelter that I have been blessed with. It also has given me the occasion to research the history of my home, namely using [this resource]( https://harvardplanning.emuseum.com/sites/924/canaday-hall), something that I have yet to explore in depth. Indeed, the only “house pride” that I now claim is that for the perfect adequacy of Canaday Hall.

### 27 – 29 April
The dearth of weblog entries this April is not because this past month has been uneventful. Part of the reason why I have not written may actually rest in one of the most significant lessons that I have learned in my first year of undergraduate studies. The realization that struck me last Friday has provided a remarkable sense of clarity and exigence for the creation of this blog post, and I have realised how many of the concerns I wrote on in my physical journal throughout this past month are linked directly with this folly.

I have spent countless hours since spring break reading and assaying to construct a mathematical model for my team of three’s final project in an introductory applied mathematics course. My choice of coupling the 2-Process model of sleep homeostatic and the circadian rhythm to environmental temperature changes continues to make this project especially difficult and time-consuming. I have allowed this project to occupy my mind excessively and disproportionately—this final project is by no means remarkably complicated. In so doing, it is likely that I have only made this endeavour more difficult and time consuming. The progress that I have made in recent days attests to this fact and to the bad habit which I will finally address directly. I have fallen into the trap of multitasking.

The folly of multitasking has been the subject of my writing before. Yet, upon reflection, a perceived lack of time for the Applied Math 50 project this past month has only been the strongest compulsion for this bad habit to have affected me most particularly in the last two weeks. I could not help but feel that my physical journal entries this past Sunday—about focusing on the environment around me whilst running or about the remarkable clarity of mind I was given on a walk on Thursday—did not adequately answer for all that could be said. Only during my run on Monday afternoon did it strike me what I had been missing. As I simply focused on the blooming leaves or other matters than schoolwork, I realised that I had been multitasking whilst running for the past month or more.

That said, there is no shame in allowing one’s labours to occupy his mind in the middle of the working day. Musing upon a particular problem when the chance arises can be helpful and is frankly unavoidable. Yet the mental multitasking that I have allowed is not of this productive kind. Beyond physical fitness and the associated benefits to one’s mind, running is an indispensable means to occupy the mind in recreation and leisure in the midst of the day. It is a chance to simply experience the natural environment in a manner similar to walking in the woods. Yet I have found myself running without *perceiving* what is around me especially in the past month or more. There is nothing wrong with occupying one’s mind with matters beyond the path in front of him to some extent. Thinking of an applied mathematics project—and a comparatively unsophisticated one for an introductory course at that—or other coursework too extensively detracts from the break and full refreshment that an activity like running provides. This is especially true when the only significant progress will occur with focused time at a desk with the materials at hand.

I have observed my fellow students distracting themselves with other coursework or news articles within my Applied Math 50 course throughout this semester, most notably during paper presentation days. Yet even when one may easily choose to do so when the choice to passively listen to “unproductive” matters is given, the benefits of dedicating one’s full attention to matters like these are manifold. It is a folly to work under conditions of distraction or half-focus, and the clarity of mind in returning to a problem after a break is deprived.

These writings may give the impression that my schedule is especially busy (relative to the standards of modern society, that is). Yet this is not at all the case. I do not live a life where multitasking is needed. The lack of clarity of mind and little focus upon matters beyond my coursework experienced in these past weeks should not have occurred. Yet after having been gifted with these realisations that I alone could not seem to have pinpointed, the clarity of mind and reinvigoration in my labours has been remarkable. From the writing of this blog post to the return of a sense of timelessness while simply observing the slices of a natural environment that I run by, the benefits of denying multitasking in all of its forms have been immediate. I cannot give enough thanks to God for this resolution. Although it may have occurred on the cusp of the end of my first year at Harvard—the last official day of classes has entered the night during the course of this writing—this may once again be one of the most significant revelations during this past year for me.

I have gone from an apparent lack of writing subjects to more to write about than this evening will permit. The next section of this extended weblog post will be dedicated to the many other lessons and notable matters regarding this past month and the end of the semester that lies ahead. Before I end, however, I must stress how the latter observations serve as a testament of God’s direct intervention in my life. It is by revealing my folly in multitasking that He has allowed me to process and ultimately write about the following matters with the clarity that they deserve. This revelation attests to how unimaginably blessed I am to concern myself with such comparatively light and personal matters at the same time.

### 30 April
The palette of greens, yellows, and reds that the budding trees now make on the landscape is becoming more monochrome as the leaves come in with ever greater vigour. I can no longer recall the last day when temperatures have fallen to what I would consider cold compared to the winter season. It is now the last day of April. The peace of mind that God has gifted me has allowed me to appreciate this beauty as I was able to perhaps one month ago. It has also reinvigorated the joy and clarity in my writing that I hope will be reflected in the small remarks that follow.

A combination of reading mathematical papers for my applied mathematics class, applying new material in multivariable calculus, and being exposed to new mathematical objects and ideas through both courses has only strengthened my affinity for what is at its core the study of order in the universe. The rigor of the mathematical proof demands a fullness and clarity of understanding that brings forth an attitude of learning to cherish. This sentiment has been reflected more concretely in a new best practice of mine that I have only discovered in the last few problems sets for Math 21a. I have begun organising and explaining my work in a way that reflects the wording of a mathematical paper. *Clarity* in the midst of the problem and especially when reviewing it results.

This month has also marked the beginning of the weather-permissive cold shower. When outdoor temperatures are at or above what I consider to be a room temperature of 16 degrees Celsius—and perhaps even lower, depending on the indoor temperature—I have rediscovered how wholesome showering with cold water is for the mind and the body. Beyond a refreshment and *clarity* that this has brought me, a certain satisfaction in reflecting aspects of human life that are now largely forgotten has accompanied this. A fire would no longer be needed to heat water for bathing when outdoor temperatures first get this warm. The weather-permissive cold shower almost emulates bathing in a natural body of water at this time of the year while undeniably saving on energy.

As I approach the end of my first year in college, some form of holistic reflection only seems appropriate. Yet it is through consistent weblog entries since February that I believe to have captured much of what I have learned already through this medium—let alone the incessant weekly physical journaling. A common theme in many of these reflections should be stressed here, however, that being the importance of choosing to live a relatively “quiet” life. By using this first year of college as an opportunity to focus fully on my studies, with running and church activities being my only two “extracurriculars,” I have reached a point of eagerness to take on a light job during the term as a means to contribute to society and to gain financial literacy. From my availability observation spreadsheet to a newfound resolution to use a reference-management platform, the value of time may be found in this all.

With one week of reading period ahead, an outline of a daily schedule and some general goals will be one of tomorrow’s tasks. More broadly, I have begun pondering goals for this summer such as reading a list of books that I have accumulated these past months or fasting for a full 24 hours. I only note these matters here, which I have only found the direction to ponder upon within the past week, as things that I should have plenty of time to think about with my schedule. It is apparent, however, that the bane of multitasking within the past month has precluded these musings.

My ability to formulate much of this weblog entry has been the result of less than 1 week of newfound focus. Throughout April, a perceived pressure to capture the passing spring through writing kept the necessity of this journal entry in the background of my mind. Yet during my run on Monday, I realised that this is something that happens naturally with focus—as my writings from this past winter and early spring may attest. The peace of God extends beyond these personal revelations, however, and is something that reflects a grace beyond comprehension. The struggles that I have faced with a matter so relatively trivial as multitasking only evidence how my life is not my own. I will end by citing an excerpt of text that I discovered with bliss on Sunday morning. The pertinence of [Proverbs 3:1-12](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs%203:1-12&version=NRSVUE) to my current writings has only now revealed itself fully to me. If my reader has gotten thus far, a brief addendum of this passage to my entry is all that I request. The clarity therein referred is in what I hope to have captured in this weblog made manifest.
