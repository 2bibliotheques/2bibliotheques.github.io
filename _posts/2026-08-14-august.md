# A Note on Perceiving the Beginnings of Seasonal Change on an Early August Afternoon
## 13 August 2026

This past Sunday afternoon, my father and I visited a certain reserve of the Cleveland Museum of Natural History that has been one of our staple walking destinations near home since before 2020. These trails pass through a rich array of ecosystems, including cold springs and dune meadows, in turn harbouring a cornucopia of rare flora and fauna. It is perhaps the singular character of this preserve that makes the change of seasons especially pronounced along its paths. The apparent sensitivity of this area to as little as one week of seasonal progression may be because one hikes through multitudinous microbiomes that respond to seasonal change in different ways. Nevertheless, I have rarely hiked within this forest without remarking that the trails look different every time.

On this particular August afternoon, however, the temporal atmosphere of late summer seemed especially pronounced. One could *sense* that it was no longer an early summer or late spring month. Unlike the more obvious changes in temperature that arrive in September mornings or the emergence of characteristically fall foliage that comes with the approach of October, the cues with which one could conclude that it was no longer June or July were subtle.

The question of what specific environmental factors would allow one to feel that he was in this specific window of time between August and early September intrigued me foremost. Upon further reflection, though, reflecting upon the very existence of this ephemeral atmosphere has yielded what I intend to record in this brief entry. Never before do I recall sensing the late summer and early fall so early in this month. Yet this perception was, once again, incredibly subtle. Here rests one of the observations worth inscribing here; I likely would not have detected these nuances if it were not for a deliberate decision to block out this time spent walking in the woods. This experience provides yet another chance to remind others and my future self, perhaps engaged in endeavours that hold one in the built environment, that taking the initiative to visit the wilderness is unfailingly worth the time.

In accord with the ever-present need to actively remember certain lessons or concepts, my principal observation from Sunday past follows this line of thought. The change of seasons never ceases to catch me off guard in some capacity. Indeed, the peculiar wilderness atmosphere of the former half of late summer exists only ephemerally. That is why “capturing” it here has been a worthwhile endeavour.

Even as I made these reflections days ago, it was not difficult to conjecture which environmental factors led to this sensation. The angle of the sun on that late afternoon, the fully-grown foliage of the forest, and a cool breeze that must have come from nearby Lake Erie (which was likely the most crucial in eliciting this observation) converged in a manner that could not occur one month in the past or one month in the future.

Wilderness no longer separates human settlements in a way briefly touched upon in [this video]( https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hAkyxgqf5Ac) from a historian who shares many more profound insights on his YouTube channel (this specific video has been hyperlinked on the “Resources” page of this web site for some time). Where most readers of this piece probably live, the role of the wilderness in the landscape has unequivocally been reversed. Yet a there may exist no less of a need for one go forth into the wilderness from time to time. What one may find here extends beyond the appreciation of the wilderness itself that has been the subject of this piece.
